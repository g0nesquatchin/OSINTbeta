Resources

A launchpad for the wider OSINT ecosystem. This tool covers continuous keyword monitoring and username lookups; the links below cover everything else.

OSINT Framework

A categorized directory of hundreds of OSINT tools — usernames, emails, domains, images, geolocation, dark web, language analysis, archives. Start here when you have a specific lead and need the right tool.

TraceLabs

Volunteer organization that runs crowdsourced OSINT events (Search Party CTFs) to help generate leads on real missing-person cases. The findings are passed to law enforcement. If you want your OSINT work to directly help locate missing people, this is the channel.

tracelabs.org ↗ Search Party CTF ↗

NCMEC CyberTipline

U.S. National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. The official reporting channel for suspected online child exploitation. Reports here go to the right law-enforcement teams.

report.cybertip.org ↗

SpiderFoot

Heavyweight automated reconnaissance against a single target (domain, IP, email, name) using ~200 modules. Runs as its own web server. Use it for deep one-target dives; use this app for continuous keyword monitoring across feeds.

Working responsibly

OSINT is powerful and easy to misuse. A few rules that keep work legitimate and useful:

  • Stick to information that's actually public — same view an anonymous visitor would have.
  • Respect platform terms of service. They differ from "legal" but matter for both you and your data's admissibility.
  • If you find evidence of a crime against a child, do not investigate further — report to NCMEC's CyberTipline or local law enforcement immediately. Continuing to dig can compromise their case.
  • Username matches and similar profiles are leads, not proof. Verify before naming anyone.
  • Keep notes of what you saw and when. Public information disappears.
